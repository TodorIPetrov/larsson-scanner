# Original User Request

## Initial Request — 2026-09-28T09:23:06Z

Research Bill Ackman's investing philosophy, principles, and track record across multiple primary and secondary sources, then synthesize findings into a concise, actionable stock-picking cheat sheet that combines quantitative screening criteria with qualitative evaluation frameworks. The deliverable should give a reader everything they need to evaluate a stock "the Ackman way" — without fluff or filler.

Working directory: ~/teamwork_projects/ackman_strategy
Integrity mode: development

## Requirements

### R1. Research Ackman's investing philosophy from diverse sources
Investigate Ackman's approach by examining his shareholder letters (Pershing Square annual letters), major public presentations and interviews, SEC filings (13F, 13D), and credible financial journalism. Identify core principles, recurring themes, and how his approach has evolved over time. Do not simply summarize a single blog post or secondary source — cross-reference and synthesize.

### R2. Produce a concise stock-picking cheat sheet
Deliver a single, well-structured document that distills the research into:
- **Core Philosophy**: Ackman's fundamental beliefs about investing (3-5 key principles)
- **Quantitative Screening Criteria**: Specific financial metrics and thresholds derived from his actual investment patterns (e.g., ROIC, free cash flow yield, debt ratios, market cap ranges)
- **Qualitative Evaluation Framework**: The non-numeric factors Ackman weighs — moat analysis, management quality, catalysts, simplicity of business model
- **Step-by-Step Screening Process**: A numbered walkthrough someone can follow to evaluate a stock candidate
- **Red Flags & Deal-Breakers**: What Ackman avoids and what has led to his worst investments
- **High-Level Track Record Summary**: Brief overview of major wins, losses, and what each teaches — focused on extracting usable lessons rather than narrative

### R3. Ensure sourcing transparency
Every major claim or principle attributed to Ackman should reference where it came from (which letter, interview, or investment). The reader should be able to trace any assertion back to a source.

## Acceptance Criteria

### Research Quality
- [ ] At least 5 distinct primary/secondary sources are referenced (shareholder letters, interviews, presentations, SEC filings, credible financial reporting)
- [ ] No principle or claim is sourced from a single source alone — key themes are corroborated across multiple sources
- [ ] The document acknowledges how Ackman's approach has evolved (e.g., pre-Valeant vs. post-Valeant adjustments)

### Cheat Sheet Completeness
- [ ] All six sections listed in R2 are present and substantive (not just placeholder headers)
- [ ] Quantitative criteria include at least 4 specific financial metrics with defined thresholds or ranges
- [ ] The step-by-step screening process has at least 5 numbered steps that are actionable (a reader could sit down and follow them)
- [ ] Red flags section includes at least 3 specific patterns or characteristics Ackman avoids, each tied to a real example from his track record

### Usability
- [ ] The full document is concise — no section exceeds what's needed to convey the point
- [ ] A reader unfamiliar with Ackman could use the screening process on a real stock after reading the document
- [ ] Financial metrics and thresholds are specific enough to be applied (not vague like "look for high returns" — instead "target ROIC > X%")

### Sourcing
- [ ] Every core principle includes a source reference (letter year, interview name, or investment example)
- [ ] The track record summary covers at least 5 notable positions (wins and losses)
