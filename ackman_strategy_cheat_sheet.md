### 1.1 The 8 Core Investment Criteria ("The Stone Tablets")
Following the $4 billion loss on Valeant Pharmaceuticals in 2015–2017, Ackman conducted a forensic audit of every gain and loss in Pershing Square’s history. He proved that 100% of the firm's catastrophic losses stemmed from compromising on business quality, tolerating high debt leverage, or underwriting complex, opaque models. To enforce permanent compliance, Ackman had the **Eight Core Investment Principles** engraved onto stone tablets and placed on the desk of every analyst `[Shane Parrish / The Knowledge Project #82; PSH Annual Reports 2018–2023]`.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                          THE PERSHING SQUARE STONE TABLETS                             │
├──────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ I.   │ Simple and predictable business                                                 │
│ II.  │ Free cash flow generative                                                       │
│ III. │ Dominant market position                                                        │
│ IV.  │ Formidable barriers to entry (Wide Economic Moat)                               │
│ V.   │ High return on invested capital (ROIC) and high incremental ROIC                │
│ VI.  │ Limited exposure to extrinsic risks beyond management control                   │
│ VII. │ Strong balance sheet, laddered debt maturities, minimal capital market need     │
│ VIII.│ Excellent management and corporate governance (or clear activist catalyst)      │
└──────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### Detailed Breakdown of the 8 Criteria:

1. **Simple and Predictable Business:**
   * *Axiom:* If an analyst cannot project normalized revenue, margins, and cash flows 5 to 10 years out with high conviction, the business fails the screen. Cash must be tangible, not an accounting artifact `[PSH 2018 Annual Report]`.
   * *Portfolio Exemplar:* **Restaurant Brands International (QSR)**. People consume coffee, burgers, and chicken predictably across economic cycles. Franchisees pay an unavoidable 4%–5% top-line royalty fee.
   * *Anti-Exemplar:* **Valeant Pharmaceuticals (VRX)**. Over 100 acquisitions obscured underlying organic trends with hundreds of pages of non-GAAP reconciliations and undisclosed captive pharmacies (Philidor).

2. **Free Cash Flow Generative:**
   * *Axiom:* Earnings are an accounting opinion; free cash flow is an economic fact. The enterprise must reliably convert operating profits into unencumbered discretionary cash flow after all maintenance capital expenditures `[PSUS Form N-2 SEC Filing]`.
   * *Portfolio Exemplar:* **Universal Music Group (UMG)**. Digital streaming platforms collect monthly subscriptions and remit royalties to UMG; cash conversion exceeds 85% of EBITDA with negligible reinvestment capital required.

3. **Dominant Market Position:**
   * *Axiom:* The target must be the undisputed #1 or #2 player in a mature, rational market structure (monopoly, duopoly, or tight oligopoly) `[PSH Investor Presentation, Feb 2024]`.
   * *Portfolio Exemplar:* **Canadian Pacific Kansas City (CPKC)**. The only single-line transcontinental railway network linking Canada, the United States, and Mexico.

4. **Formidable Barriers to Entry (Wide Moat):**
   * *Axiom:* The economic, legal, or physical cost for a well-funded competitor to replicate the company's asset base or network must be insurmountable `[PSH 2020 Annual Report]`.
   * *Portfolio Exemplar:* **Hilton Worldwide (HLT)**. Over 170 million Hilton Honors members, global brand trust, and a worldwide reservation network make independent hotel replication economically irrational.

5. **High Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) & Incremental ROIC:**
   * *Axiom:* Compounding is governed by Charlie Munger’s law: a stock’s long-term return converges to the underlying business's return on capital. The business must generate ROIC $\ge 15.0\%$, and incremental dollars reinvested must earn $\ge 20.0\%-25.0\%+$ `[Lex Fridman #413; PSH 2022 Annual Report]`.
   * *Portfolio Exemplar:* **Chipotle Mexican Grill (CMG)**. New store build cost is ~$1.2M, producing ~$3.0M Average Unit Volume (AUV) at 26%+ restaurant margins, generating cash-on-cash unit returns $>50\%-60\%$.

6. **Limited Exposure to Extrinsic Risks:**
   * *Axiom:* The enterprise’s survival and intrinsic compounding must be dictated by management execution and structural demand, never by uncontrollable external macro variables `[PSH 2021 Annual Report]`.
   * *Screening Exclusions:* Direct commodity producers (oil & gas E&Ps, miners, agricultural growers), commercial banks with asset-liability mismatch risk, and early-stage biotech with binary clinical trial hurdles.

7. **Strong Balance Sheet & Minimal Capital Markets Dependency:**
   * *Axiom:* A company that requires continuous access to debt or equity capital markets to stay solvent is an existential hazard. The balance sheet must withstand severe credit freezes without diluting shareholders `[PSUS Form N-2 Filing]`.
   * *Portfolio Exemplar:* **Alphabet (GOOGL)** and **Meta Platforms (META)**. Massive net-cash balance sheets (>$50B–$100B cash & equivalents) generating tens of billions in annual organic free cash flow post-capex.

8. **Excellent Management & Governance (or Actionable Activist Catalyst):**
   * *Axiom:* Leadership must exhibit owner-orientation, integrity, and strict capital allocation discipline. If management is suboptimal, there must be a clear path for Pershing Square to install world-class operators and restructure incentives `[PSH 2023 Annual Report]`.
   * *Portfolio Exemplar:* Installing **Brian Niccol** at Chipotle (2018) and **E. Hunter Harrison** at Canadian Pacific (2012).

### 1.2 Concentrated Portfolio Architecture
Ackman operates an ultra-concentrated portfolio structure that runs counter to modern portfolio theory `[PSH Annual Reports 2014–2023; PSUS Prospectus]`:

* **Position Count:** Exactly **8 to 12 core holdings** (rarely expanding to 15).
* **Capital Sizing:** The top 5 holdings represent **50% to 75%** of total net asset value (NAV). A standard initial core stake is sized at **8% to 15%** of fund capital.
* **Large/Mega-Cap Bias:** Minimum market cap hurdle of **$10 Billion**, with the overwhelming majority invested in **$25 Billion to $1.0+ Trillion** enterprises. Scale ensures liquidity, operational stability, and dominant market positioning.
* **Low Portfolio Turnover:** Average holding periods range from **3 to 7+ years** (Restaurant Brands held >10 years; Chipotle held >8 years; Hilton held >8 years). Portfolio turnover is typically $<15\%-25\%$ annually.

### 1.3 Asymmetric Risk/Reward & Tail-Risk Macro Hedging
Ackman rejects traditional stock shorting and standard portfolio diversification as risk mitigation tools. Standalone short selling carries mathematically asymmetric downside (100% maximum gain vs. infinite potential loss), negative carry (borrow fees and dividends), and vulnerability to squeezes `[PSH 2021 Annual Report Letter]`.

Instead, Pershing Square manages downside risk via **asymmetric tail-risk macro hedges**:
* **Structural Convexity:** Hedges are constructed using out-of-the-money derivatives (Credit Default Swaps on corporate credit indices or interest rate swaptions on U.S. Treasuries). Premium costs are capped at a low annual carry bleed ($<1\%-2\%$ of NAV).
* **Convex Payouts:** During a black-swan market dislocation, payoffs reach **50x to 100x** the capital invested.
* **The Liquidity Engine Heuristic:** The primary objective of the macro hedge is not speculative profit; it is to generate massive liquidity at the absolute trough of a panic to purchase high-quality compounders at generational valuation lows `[PSH 2020 Annual Report; 2022 Annual Report]`.

### 2.2 10-K Formula Extraction & Computation Guide for Analysts

#### Metric 1: Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)
$$\text{ROIC} = \frac{\text{NOPAT}}{\text{Average Invested Capital}} = \frac{\text{Operating Income (EBIT)} \times (1 - \text{Effective Tax Rate})}{(\text{Current Portion of Debt} + \text{Total Long-Term Debt} + \text{Total Stockholders' Equity}) - \text{Cash \& Equivalents}}$$

* **10-K Source:** Income Statement (Operating Income, Income Tax Provision); Balance Sheet (Current Debt, Long-term Debt, Stockholders' Equity, Cash & Cash Equivalents).
* **Analytical Rule:** Strip out non-operating one-time gains from EBIT. Standardize debt to include both current maturities and long-term debt.
* **The Negative Equity Asset-Light Rule:** For asset-light royalty and franchise compounders (e.g., Hilton, Domino's, AutoZone) where extensive historical share buybacks have pushed GAAP Stockholders' Equity negative, computing Invested Capital via the financing side artificially compresses the denominator. In such cases, compute Invested Capital from operating assets: $\text{Invested Capital} = \text{Net Working Capital (ex-cash)} + \text{Net PP&E} + \text{Capitalized Intangibles}$, OR add back cumulative treasury stock / repurchases to equity.
* **Target:** Minimum $\ge 15.0\%$ sustained across a 5-year cycle. Target $20.0\%-45.0\%+$ for asset-light compounders `[Lowe's 45% Target, PSH 2022 Annual Report]`.

#### Metric 2: Free Cash Flow (FCF) Conversion & FCF Yield
$$\text{FCF Conversion} = \frac{\text{Cash Flow from Operations} - \text{Capital Expenditures}}{\text{GAAP Net Income}}$$
$$\text{Equity FCF Yield} = \frac{\text{Free Cash Flow (FCFE)}}{\text{Market Capitalization}}$$
$$\text{Enterprise FCF Yield} = \frac{\text{Unlevered Free Cash Flow (FCFF)}}{\text{Enterprise Value}}$$
Where:
$$\text{Enterprise Value} = \text{Market Capitalization} + \text{Total Funded Debt} - \text{Total Cash \& Equivalents}$$

* **10-K Source:** Statement of Cash Flows (Net cash provided by operating activities, Additions to property and equipment / Capex); Income Statement (Net Income); 10-K Cover Page (Shares Outstanding × Stock Price).
* **Analytical Rule:** FCF conversion must be $\ge 85.0\%$. In asset-light models with negative working capital (where customers pay upfront, e.g., Hilton/QSR), conversion regularly exceeds $100.0\%$. Canadian Pacific Kansas City established a post-merger commitment to $>90\%$ FCF conversion `[PSH Investor Day, Feb 2024]`. Note on Inflection Candidates: For high-growth or transitioning platforms (e.g., Netflix in 2021), historical conversion may be depressed by heavy content/capex investments; these must be rigorously underwritten on a forward 3-5 year cash inflection curve.

#### Metric 3: Leverage & Solvency Ratios
$$\text{Net Debt / EBITDA} = \frac{(\text{Current Portion of Debt} + \text{Long-Term Debt}) - \text{Cash \& Equivalents}}{\text{Operating Income (EBIT)} + \text{Depreciation \& Amortization}}$$
$$\text{Interest Coverage Ratio} = \frac{\text{GAAP Operating Income (EBIT)}}{\text{Gross Interest Expense}}$$

* **10-K Source:** Balance Sheet (Current and Long-Term Debt, Cash); Income Statement (Operating Income, Interest Expense); Statement of Cash Flows (D&A).
* **Thresholds:**
  * *Standard Operating Firms:* Net Debt / EBITDA $\le 2.5\text{x} - 3.0\text{x}$.
  * *Asset-Light Franchise Exception:* Net Debt / EBITDA bounded up to $\le 4.5\text{x}$, strictly conditioned on:
    1. Over 85% of Operating Profit / EBITDA (or net revenues excluding ASC 606 pass-through reimbursed costs) derived from contracted franchise/royalty fees and system management contracts.
    2. Over 80%–85%+ fixed-rate debt (including variable-rate debt synthetically converted to fixed via interest rate swaps disclosed in 10-K Note 11), with no debt maturity cliff exceeding 15%–18% of total debt in any single year, and minimal maturities (<5%) due within 12 months.
    3. Stressed Interest Coverage remaining $\ge 4.0\text{x}-5.0\text{x}$ (EBIT / Gross Interest).

## 3. Qualitative Evaluation Framework (The Moat & Governance Screen)

No valuation discount can compensate for a structurally broken business model. Pershing Square uses a **6-dimension qualitative filter** prior to financial modeling `[PSH 2018–2023 Annual Reports; PSUS Prospectus]`.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                  THE PERSHING SQUARE 6-DIMENSION QUALITATIVE SCREEN                    │
├───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Durable Economic Moat          │ Irreplaceable assets, network effects, IP catalogs │
│ 2. Uncompromising Pricing Power   │ Pass-through inflation; inelastic demand; royalties│
│ 3. Asset-Light / Royalty Dynamics │ Negative working capital; zero franchisee capex drag│
│ 4. Governance & Capital Discipline│ ROIC incentive compensation; aggressive buybacks   │
│ 5. Extrinsic Risk Insularity      │ Immune to commodity, macro, or regulatory shifts   │
│ 6. Activist Catalyst Levers       │ Operational gaps, CEO replacement, spin-offs       │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### 3.1 Dimension 1: Economic Moats & Barriers to Entry
Pershing Square requires businesses that operate inside structural economic fortresses:
* **Irreplaceable Physical Infrastructure:** Transcontinental rail lines (CPKC) and master-planned community land banks (Howard Hughes Holdings). Replicating these assets is economically and legally impossible.
* **Two-Sided Network Effects:** Marketplaces where supply density drives consumer liquidity, creating insurmountable switching costs (Uber Technologies, Meta Platforms with 3.3B+ daily users).
* **Intellectual Property & Copyright Tollbooths:** Multi-decade rights to iconic master music recordings (Universal Music Group). No capital deployment can recreate the last 80 years of human music history.
* **Scale-Driven Cost Advantages:** Massive volume purchasing power that competitors cannot match (Lowe's and Home Depot duopoly in home improvement).

### 3.2 Dimension 2: Pricing Power & Inflation Pass-Through
According to Ackman, pricing power is the primary hallmark of business greatness `[Lex Fridman #413]`:
* **Small Ticket Size Relative to Value Delivered:** Chipotle’s average entree price (~$10–$11) allows $0.50 price hikes (5%) without customer churn. The absolute cost to the diner is negligible, but it drops directly to store-level margins to offset wage and ingredient inflation.
* **Mission-Critical Essentiality:** ADP payroll services represent a fraction of corporate overhead, but payroll processing errors create severe legal and operational liabilities; clients rarely switch over modest price increases.
* **Top-Line Contractual Royalties:** Hilton and Restaurant Brands collect contractual percentages (4%–5.5%) of **gross sales**. When franchisees raise menu prices or room rates (RevPAR) to offset inflation, franchisor revenue rises automatically with zero lag and zero incremental capital expenditure.

### 3.3 Dimension 3: Asset-Light & Royalty Dynamics
Pershing Square favors businesses that function as **"royalties on the growth of the global economy"**:
* **Franchise Economics:** Hilton and QSR license intellectual property and systems to independent franchisees. The franchisee provides 100% of the capital expenditure, takes on property-level debt, and absorbs store-level labor and utility inflation.
* **Negative Working Capital:** Customers pay with cash or credit card immediately upon service delivery, while suppliers are paid on 30- to 60-day terms. The business generates cash before paying its bills, funding growth organically.
* **Maintenance Capex Discipline:** Maintenance capex is typically $<2\%-3\%$ of revenues.

### 3.4 Dimension 4: Management Alignment & Capital Allocation
Pershing Square conducts forensic audits of the proxy statement (DEF 14A):
* **Compensation Structure:** Management incentives must be tied to **per-share intrinsic compounding** (ROIC, Free Cash Flow per Share, relative Total Shareholder Return) rather than unadjusted revenue growth or accounting EBITDA.
* **Significant Skin-in-the-Game:** Executives must possess meaningful personal stock ownership to ensure alignment with outside shareholders.
* **Capital Allocation Discipline:** Surplus cash must be returned via **counter-cyclical share repurchases** when the stock trades at a discount to intrinsic value, rather than squandered on dilutive, empire-building M&A.

### 3.5 Dimension 5: Extrinsic Risk Insularity
A business must be insulated from external macroeconomic forces beyond management's control:
* **No Direct Commodity Risk:** The company must not be an oil/gas producer, miner, or agricultural producer.
* **No Regulatory Vulnerability:** The business model must not rely on regulatory loopholes, captive specialty pharmacies, or high-risk multi-level marketing structures.
* **No Refinancing Squeeze:** Operations must not depend on continuous access to credit markets.

### 3.6 Dimension 6: Identifiable Value-Creation Catalysts (The 5 Activist Levers)
When entering an underperforming business, Pershing Square identifies specific operational levers to unlock value `[PSH 2021 Annual Report]`:
1. **Forensic Benchmarking:** Identify a quantifiable performance gap against the best-in-class industry peer (e.g., Canadian Pacific's 81% Operating Ratio vs. Canadian National's 65%; Lowe's 9% EBIT margin vs. Home Depot's 14.5%).
2. **Board Reconstitution:** Secure board seats cordially and install independent directors who are industry operators.
3. **CEO Replacement:** Recruit proven industry leaders (e.g., Brian Niccol to Chipotle, Hunter Harrison to Canadian Pacific, Marvin Ellison to Lowe's).
4. **Incentive Redesign:** Overhaul executive compensation packages to reward ROIC and FCF per share.
5. **Portfolio Rationalization:** Shut down loss-making experiments, divest non-core assets, and focus capital on the core moat.

### Anti-Pattern 1: Debt-Fueled Serial M&A & Non-GAAP Obfuscation (Valeant — $4.0B Loss)
* **The Trap:** Companies that generate headline "growth" through relentless debt-funded acquisitions while presenting heavily adjusted "Cash EPS" or "Adjusted EBITDA" that strips out recurring amortizations, restructuring charges, and legal expenses `[Lex Fridman #413; PSH 2016 Annual Report]`.
* **The Failure Mechanism:** Serial M&A hides organic revenue declines and product defects. When access to cheap debt markets terminates or regulatory scrutiny halts price gouging, the equity is wiped out by senior debt obligations.
* **The Screening Rule:** **Reject any company where M&A contributes >20% of revenue growth over 3 years, Net Debt/EBITDA exceeds 4.0x, or Non-GAAP Adjusted Earnings diverge from GAAP Operating Cash Flow by >25%.**

### Anti-Pattern 2: Activist Short Selling & Regulatory Dependency (Herbalife — ~$1.0B Loss)
* **The Trap:** Attempting to profit from shorting an overvalued or structurally flawed company by waging a public campaign and relying on regulatory enforcement `[PSH 2017 Annual Report; FT 2022]`.
* **The Failure Mechanism:** Short selling carries an unfavorable mathematical profile: maximum upside is 100%, while downside is mathematically infinite. Borrow costs, margin requirements, and dividend payments create a persistent negative carry. Rival investors (e.g., Carl Icahn) can engineer a short squeeze, while regulatory agencies rarely deliver corporate death penalties.
* **The Screening Rule:** **Never engage in activist short selling. The asset class was officially and permanently retired from Pershing Square in 2022.**

### Anti-Pattern 3: Secular Retail Decline & Broken Moat Turnarounds (JCPenney / Borders)
* **The Trap:** Investing in legacy brick-and-mortar retail businesses suffering from secular decline, believing real estate value or a charismatic executive can execute a turnaround `[Track Record Report; PSH 2013 Letter]`.
* **The Failure Mechanism:** Retail turnarounds rarely succeed because customer shopping habits are difficult to shift once lost to structural competitors (Amazon, digital streaming). Furthermore, long-term, non-cancellable retail store leases act as rigid debt liabilities during sales downturns.
* **The Screening Rule:** **Avoid companies facing secular technological substitution. Do not underwrite retail turnarounds that violate established customer pricing habits.**

### Anti-Pattern 4: Sudden Loss of Business Model Predictability (Netflix — $400M Loss Exit)
* **The Trap:** Holding or averaging down on an investment when core operating metrics become volatile and management abruptly alters the business model `[PSH Letter to Investors, April 20, 2022]`.
* **The Failure Mechanism:** In April 2022, Netflix announced subscriber declines and guided toward an advertising tier. Ackman sold the entire $1.1B stake within 24 hours at a $400M loss, recognizing that the dispersion of future cash flow outcomes had widened beyond acceptable underwriting bounds.
* **The Screening Rule (The Thesis Invalidation Rule):** **If new data impairs the core predictability of 5-year revenues and margins, liquidate the position immediately. Never average down on an invalidated thesis.**
